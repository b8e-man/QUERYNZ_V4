Mask a Gazette notice's title as well as its text and publish a title cut before any property it names: possession notices title themselves with the property's address, and a lender's name can run on from the borrowers'.
area: Gazette notices, mortgagee possession, masking samples, published titles, hard limits 11 and 15
paid-for-by: 2026-10-20, gazette session 5, step 5.3

Counting the possession notices' titles to see their variety printed a
handful whole, and five named a property by street address: a title is not
safe because it is a title. The mask lesson (vocabulary, not case) applies
to every field printed, titles included, and digits are masked whatever
the vocabulary says. Two more traps from the same notices. The old capture
runs words together at a bracket or a colon ("(“the mortgage”):GE
Custodians"), so a backward walk for a name takes the borrowers' names and
the lender's as one run ("in the matter of <persons> <lender> Limited");
flag a run that starts after "in the matter of" or "To:" and publish only
the end of it the companies register holds. And a list of unmatched names
printed to judge a join prints whatever the reader got wrong, people
included: print only names ending in a legal suffix, or their shapes.
