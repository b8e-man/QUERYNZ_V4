A source's kind field is a hint, never the profile: recognise every document by its own shape, measured on the whole holding, before building a reader for it.
area: readers, profiles, mcpsource, courts

Why: the ingest MCP's courts corpus files every PDF of a case page as `judgment_pdf`. Measured on the 5,611 records, one in six of those files is a media release, a hearing transcript, counsel's submission or a case synopsis, and 497 judgments sit under opaque Judicial Decisions Online names. A reader built on the kind field would have parsed captions out of 450 documents that have none, and recorded each as a judgment "unparsed". Recognising by shape (a court line, numbered paragraphs, a JUDGMENT heading; a `[year] NZSC Trans n` line and speaker labels; SUBMISSIONS/CHRONOLOGY on the first page; a press header) put every file under its own profile first, and the reader for each came after (profiles before readers).

How to apply: when a source carries a type, kind or category field, treat it as one signal among the shape's. Write the recognition from the file's first page alone, run it over every record held, and look at the "none" and the smallest buckets by hand before writing any reader: the small buckets are where the field lies. Record the counts per profile in the profile's signals so the next session sees what the recognition was measured on. A regex over a court's name wants an explicit alternation: a lazy `[A-Z ]+?` matched "COURT" inside "COURT OF APPEAL" and sent 315 Court of Appeal judgments to the generic profile until the counts were read.
