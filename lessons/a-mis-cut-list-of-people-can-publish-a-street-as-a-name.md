Set aside a person's record when a name field reads as an address (two words ending in Street, Road, …, or a digit): a mis-cut list of people otherwise publishes a street as a name.
area: Gazette notices, bankruptcies, reading documents, hard limit 15, masking samples, people
paid-for-by: 2026-10-20, gazette session 3, step 4.1

The bankruptcy lists print "Surname, Given, <address> – <day>." When a record
is cut wrong, the surname or given-names field can be the street ("Sample
Street") and the published view, which never carries an address column,
carries one anyway. Counting name fields by case class only (no letters
shown) found 82 surnames ending in a thoroughfare word among 65,000. A
single word stays a surname (Lane, Court, Way, Close are, as the celebrant
lesson says); two words ending in Street, Road, Avenue, Drive, Place,
Crescent, Terrace, Highway and the like, or any digit, set the record
aside. Looking was done by a shape mask (CAP, Cap, low per word): the
vocabulary mask still leaked common given names, which recur across
hundreds of lists, so for notices about people show a capitalised word only
where its lower case is a template word too.
