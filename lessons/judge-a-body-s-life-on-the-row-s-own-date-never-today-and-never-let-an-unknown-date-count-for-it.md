Judge whether a body was alive on the row's own date, never today, and never let a missing date count in a body's favour: 33 live rows name a company whose only live namesake today was registered after the row, and the right one is gone.
area: links, rules, organisation-sole-company, organisation-one-live-company, bodies, dates

Why: ruling 125 (30 October 2026) widened the organisation rules from donor rows to every organisation-named row, and the first measurement showed why "alive" had to move with the row: an interests return of 2016 naming Ivory Limited, a disclosure of 2022 naming Christopher & Banks Ltd, each has one company of the name live today, and it is the wrong one, registered years after the row; the right one was removed in between. Ruling 114's @2 rule also let a row with no date confirm, because `_live` read an unknown date as "not counting against": every one of the 1,170 undated holder rows would have confirmed to whatever body stood today.

How to apply: a time-aware rule takes the row's own window (`started_on` .. `ended_on`) and asks the register's dates whether the body was registered by the window's end and not removed before its start; a row with no date of its own gets no confirmation and a reason that says "undated", and the view is asked to publish the date (a card to its lane) rather than the rule guessing one. Keep a test in which the body live today is not the one alive on the row's date, so the rule can never quietly go back to "today".
