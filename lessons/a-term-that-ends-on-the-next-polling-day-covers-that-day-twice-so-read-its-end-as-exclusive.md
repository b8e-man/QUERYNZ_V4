A term that ends on the next polling day covers that day twice: read the end as exclusive, or every re-elected member is two members on polling day and a day-based join stays near for them.

`pub.members` dates each term from its polling day (`from_on`) to the
next polling day (`to_by`); no row carries `ended_on`. So a member
re-elected in 2023 has a ge-2020 term ending 14 October 2023 and a
ge-2023 term starting 14 October 2023. A window test with both ends
inclusive finds two member rows of the name on that day, which a
one-member rule reads as two people and leaves near; worse, a rule that
counts the windows a row falls in can confirm to either. The rule reads
a term as from its start up to, not on, its end (`rules.sitting`), so
one person's two terms never both cover a day; the test pins the
polling day to the new term. Ask of any pair of periods built from
"the next one's start": which side owns the shared day.

The same build showed the other edge: a row dated before the first term
a view holds (the written questions from 2003, the diaries from 2017) is
no evidence of "no member sitting". It makes no link and is counted
apart, because the view could not have seen the member.
