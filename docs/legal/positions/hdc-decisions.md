# Health and Disability Commissioner decisions (draft position, ruling 61)

hosts: hdc.org.nz

Class: the Health and Disability Commissioner's published decisions (opinions
on complaints under the Health and Disability Commissioner Act 1994 and the
Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers' Rights) and case studies,
from 2017. Lane: `registers/hdc_decisions`. Draft, held in the lane's folder
because the fence keeps a build session out of `docs/legal/positions/`; the
integrator moves it there. Read 21 October 2026 by hdc-1.

**Finding: open and lawful to copy for research; any other use needs the
Commissioner's written permission. HELD for capture into the estate and for
any product until that permission (or Arthur's ruling) is on file as an
`authorised:` line on this page.**

1. **Is the page open?** Yes. The listings (`/decisions/search-decisions/?filter-year=Y&page=N`,
   `/decisions/case-studies-nga-matai-take/`), every decision page and the
   pages about the site answered 200 to a plain request, no login, lock or
   challenge (36 requests, 5 s apart, 21 October 2026). The site has no
   robots file (404), so no crawl delay is stated.
2. **Is storing it lawful copying?** For research, yes. The copyright page
   (`d9686c4a…`, `/copyright/`): the material belongs to the Commissioner;
   3.1 "may be reproduced for personal, educational or research purposes
   without formal permission or charge"; 3.2 "must not be used for any other
   purpose without the prior written permission"; 3.3 any use is sourced to
   the site with the date of publication, reproduced accurately, never in a
   misleading context; the logo never. A question for the lawyer: whether an
   opinion under Part 4 of the Act is a report of a statutory inquiry with no
   copyright (Copyright Act 1994, s 27(1)); the lane does not rely on it.
3. **What terms did we accept?** The copyright terms above and the
   disclaimer (`0e1f55e1…`, `/terms-conditions/`: accuracy not guaranteed,
   material may include third parties' views). The estate's purpose includes
   a product that may be sold (use case 11), which is "any other purpose"
   under 3.2. **So the capture as briefed (every decision kept, rows shown in
   a template) waits on written permission.** The proof landed for building
   (about thirty pages in the sample store) is research copying under 3.1.
4. **What does privacy law, or the statute, limit?** The decisions are about
   consumers' health care. The Commissioner removes names ("Names have been
   removed … to protect privacy", letter labels assigned in order) and names
   providers under its Policy for Naming Providers (`3d61c427…`, June 2023):
   Health NZ, its districts and public hospitals generally; other group
   providers and individuals case by case, an individual only on stated
   grounds and after any disciplinary process. The End of Life Choice Act
   2019, s 36, bars publishing who administered assisted dying medication and
   their employer. The Privacy Act 2020 and the Health Information Privacy
   Code 2020: a de-identified consumer can be re-identified from a
   description, a date and a provider. So the lane keeps no heading (it
   describes the care), no paragraph, no consumer, family member or letter
   label, no age, condition or place beyond the provider, no practitioner's
   name (a named practitioner is kept as a role and the policy's ground), no
   adviser or staff, and no case study's address (its slug is its summary).
   Every kept text passes a guard that refuses a consumer detail. A finding is
   the Commissioner's opinion, not a court's. A page the Commissioner takes
   down leaves our views at the next load.

Crawl delay: none stated (no robots file). Floor: one request at a time, at
least 5 s apart, off-peak (22:00 to 06:00 NZ time), each year's listing
walked weekly (about 85 pages for 2017 to 2026), each decision fetched once
and again only when its tile changes; a 403, 429, any 5xx or a challenge
stops the run. Author: hdc-1 (Opus), 21 October 2026. A lawyer reviews this
page before anything is sold.
