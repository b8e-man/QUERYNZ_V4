# OpenLaw NZ case law (draft position, ruling 61)

hosts: openlaw.nz

Class: the parsed case law served by OpenLaw NZ (a registered charity,
CC55967) through its GraphQL interface at `api.openlaw.nz/graphql`, and the
pages about it on `www.openlaw.nz`. The interface's terms
(`assets.openlaw.nz/apiterms.pdf`) sit on a shared S3 host that this page
does not cover: the integrator dropped it at the merge, and the one
document is landed by hand if the lane is ever authorised. Lane:
`registers/openlaw`.

Draft, written in the lane's folder because the fence keeps a build
session out of `docs/legal/positions/`, and moved here by the integrator
at the merge of `openlaw-1`. Read 21 October 2026 through the fetch gate by

**Finding: the terms we accepted bar the capture as briefed.** Until
OpenLaw NZ authorises it in writing, the lane holds no case record, builds
no table from one and publishes nothing. See question 3.

1. **Is the page open?** Yes, with our own key. The interface asks for an
   `x-apikey` header; the key is the one Arthur generated by signing up on
   15 August 2026 (`/api-key`: "sign up ... to instantly generate a
   personal key"), held in the credential folder. No challenge, block or
   CAPTCHA was met. `openlaw.nz` serves `robots.txt` as `User-agent: *` /
   `Disallow:` (allow all, no crawl delay); `api.openlaw.nz` answers 404
   for it. Two introspection requests (the schema, no case content)
   answered 200 on 21 October 2026.
2. **Is storing it lawful copying?** For the judgments themselves, yes:
   the Copyright Act 1994, section 27(1), gives no copyright in the
   judgments of a court or tribunal, and OpenLaw says the same ("case law is
   not subject to copyright restrictions", our-mission, `36cbe0b5…`). The
   judgments come from the Ministry of Justice's published PDFs (High
   Court, Court of Appeal, Supreme Court from about 2004, and ACC appeal
   decisions of the District Court). What OpenLaw adds (the text
   conversion, the citation graph, the statute links, the party table) is
   its own work and may be a compilation in copyright; lawyer to say how
   far. But the binding limit is the contract below, not copyright.
3. **What terms did we accept?** The OpenLaw NZ API Terms of Use
   (`apiterms.pdf`, `d327cea0…`), accepted by signing up for the key:
   - 1.2: a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to use the
     interface, on these terms;
   - **3.6(a): without OpenLaw's express authorisation we may not "scrape,
     build databases, or otherwise create permanent copies" of content
     returned by the interface.** Landing every case record in the
     document store and building registers from it is exactly that;
   - 3.6(b): nor copy, modify, create a derivative work of, distribute or
     publicly display it, or sublicense it;
   - 3.7: without express authorisation, no charging for access to a
     service built using the interface;
   - 3.2: OpenLaw may set limits; we may not get around them;
   - 5.1: the terms may change at any time; continued use accepts them;
   - 5.3(b): on ending the terms, every stored item must be deleted
     unless OpenLaw agrees otherwise, which collides with our rule that a
     captured document is never deleted.
   The our-mission page (`36cbe0b5…`) adds that the database and interface
   are "only for non-commercial purposes", that a commercial use benefiting
   access to legal information "we will consider - please get in touch"
   (`enquiries@openlaw.nz`), and that the website's own search may be used
   for any purpose. So: transient use (introspection, counting the cases,
   reading a page in memory for a test) is within the licence; a permanent
   copy, a register built from it, a published view, and any sale are not,
   until OpenLaw authorises them in writing. Asking is Arthur's (a letter
   to an outside body). The decision queue holds it, marked no default.
4. **What does privacy law, or a statute, limit?** A judgment names people:
   parties, witnesses, sometimes victims. Suppression binds every product
   (plan 5.4; hard limit 16): the Criminal Procedure Act 2011, sections 200
   to 211 (a court's order suppressing a defendant's, witness's or victim's
   identity; automatic suppression for complainants in specified sexual
   cases and for child complainants and witnesses; breach an offence), and
   the publication limits of the family jurisdictions (Family Court
   proceedings, Oranga Tamariki, care of children), lawyer to list in
   full. The Ministry publishes with orders applied and OpenLaw leaves out
   "some suppressed decisions"; an order made or widened after publication
   is not seen by either. So the lane flags a case from the text's own
   suppression and anonymisation markers and withholds every non-Crown
   party name and the title of a flagged case on every surface, keeping the
   row, its role and the case; an anonymised party ("W", "Mr X") is never
   expanded. The Privacy Act 2020 applies to what we hold: a judgment is a
   publicly available publication, but use must still be fair and
   accurate, and a request to correct or a court's later order needs a
   takedown path before any product shows a person's case. No address is
   read or kept (hard limit 15); the reading takes names and roles only,
   never counsel, witnesses or the text's narrative.
