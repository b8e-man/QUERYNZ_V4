Subject: Request for authorisation under your API terms (clauses 3.6 and 3.7)

Kia ora OpenLaw NZ team,

I run Follow the Money (followthemoney.nz), a New Zealand public-records
service that links registers such as companies, charities, political
donations and Gazette notices so that a person can see who is connected to
whom, with every statement cited to its source. I hold an OpenLaw API key,
issued on 15 August 2026.

I am writing to ask for your express authorisation, under clauses 3.6 and
3.7 of your API Terms of Use, to do three things with the case records your
interface returns:

1. Keep a verbatim copy of each case record on our own servers and build
   tables from it (the parties, the citations between judgments, and the
   statutes a judgment applies), rather than query the interface afresh
   each time.
2. Show parts of those records in our product: the citation, court and
   date, the parties with suppression orders applied, the judgments cited,
   and the statutes linked, each attributed to OpenLaw NZ with a link to the
   case on openlaw.nz. The product is free today; parts of it may later be
   paid for, which is why I ask under clause 3.7 as well.
3. Keep the copy if the terms end (clause 5.3(b)), because our records
   policy is that a captured document is never deleted, only withdrawn
   from publication.

What I offer in return: attribution of OpenLaw NZ on every surface that
shows your data; full compliance with suppression (an anonymised or
suppressed party is never named on any surface, and a case the text flags
as suppressed is shown by citation only); and a polite pace. We would read
the roughly 46,500 cases through about 4,700 requests, one at a time, three
seconds apart, overnight, over two or three nights, and then only new
cases nightly.

If you would prefer a different arrangement, such as a bulk file, a licence
fee, or a narrower set of fields, I am glad to discuss it. The judgments
themselves are Ministry of Justice publications; it is your conversion,
parties table, citation graph and statute links that I am asking to use.

Ngā mihi,

Arthur
(arthur@betelgeuse.co.nz)
