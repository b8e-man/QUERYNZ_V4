# Whether to ask OpenLaw NZ for written permission to copy its case records (its API terms forbid copying them without it)
default: no default. The lane is built and held: it asks for no case record and lands nothing until the position page carries OpenLaw's written authorisation (an `authorised:` line). Hard limit 4: the terms we accepted when the key was issued bar the capture as briefed. OpenLaw NZ API Terms of Use, clause 3.6(a): without OpenLaw's express authorisation, no "scrape, build databases, or otherwise create permanent copies" of what the interface returns. 3.6(b) bars public display and derivative works. 3.7 bars charging for a service built on it. 5.3(b) says every stored item is deleted when the terms end, and our rule is that a captured document is never deleted. OpenLaw's mission page says the interface is "only for non-commercial purposes" and that it will consider a commercial use that helps access to legal information.
blocks: step 5.4 item 1 (the OpenLaw lane's capture, load, `pub.court_cases` and the three other views, and its jobs); the legal template's court-judgment section, where it would read OpenLaw.
recommendation: Arthur writes to OpenLaw at enquiries@openlaw.nz. He asks for written authorisation to (a) keep a verbatim copy of each case record and build tables from it, (b) show parts of it (citation, court, date, parties with suppression applied, citations and statute links) in a product that may later be paid for, and (c) keep that copy if the terms end. He offers attribution, suppression compliance and our pace (one request at a time, 3 s apart, at night, 46,545 cases in about 4,700 requests over two or three nights). A letter to an outside body is Arthur's. If OpenLaw says no, or does not answer: drop the lane. The judgments themselves have no copyright (Copyright Act 1994, s 27(1)) and come from the Ministry of Justice. The courts lane (5.4 item 2) and a direct capture of the courts' own published judgments cover the same ground without these terms, though without OpenLaw's citation graph and statute links.
